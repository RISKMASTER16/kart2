      STEP 1 OF 3 · THE IDEA

      Better cache choices,
      hours ahead.

      A cache is fast storage for copies of articles. It has limited room. We test whether a model can choose better articles than a simple rule before readers arrive.

      WHAT WE COMPARE

      A simple rule. A learned model.

      THE BASELINE

      Standard rule

      Assume the next hour looks like the latest observed hour. Keep the articles that were most popular then.

      OUR PREDICTOR

      Our model (XGBoost)

      Learn patterns in past views, including daily rhythms and how attention fades. Predict demand for the future hour.

      Both can be tested one, two, three or six hours ahead. We compare their cache choices at the same planning time and capacity.

      1. 01

        Use only the past.

        Both methods see earlier page views. The hour being tested is still in the future.

      2. 02

        Choose the cache.

        Each method ranks articles and selects 100,000 to keep in fast storage.

      3. 03

        Check what happened.

        Replay actual views in the later hour. Count the share that would find their article already stored.

      This is a historical experiment, not a live change to Wikipedia's servers.

        What this doesn't prove.

        This is one short historical test. It doesn't mean we can predict breaking news or replace Wikipedia's live caching system.

        Read the limitations
        • Only a few days were evaluated; other weeks and languages may differ.
        • Our denominator is the top 150,000 English Wikipedia articles each hour.
        • Storage choices change once an hour and assume equal article sizes.
        • Unexpected first-hour surges may have no advance signal.
        • Statistical uncertainty describes this test period, not every possible future day.
        • Traffic that resembles bots was flagged, not removed.

        How to read this comparison

        The filled blocks show relative article capacity. Each block stands for 10,000 article spaces. The bars show recorded views served from fast storage on a scale from zero to 100 percent.

        Values per 1,000 are rounded illustrations of the average hit rates. Both sizes use the same 72 hours at a one hour planning time. A page view is counted in the retained top 150,000 English Wikipedia articles for that hour.

        Each article counts as one space. This is not a comparison of storage in bytes, hardware price or financial savings. Bigger storage helped in this test; it does not establish the best cost tradeoff.
